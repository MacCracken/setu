# `cyrius distlib`'s `dist/<lib>.deps` sidecar under-reports, and is neither declaration-driven nor a complete inference

**Status:** 🔴 **OPEN — cyrius defect, filed from setu 0.8.2.** Filed 2026-08-07.
**Cross-repo:** cyrius (`cyrius distlib`) + setu (the lib that exposed it) + aethersafha / crab (the
consumers it misled). Mirror of `cyrius/docs/development/issues/2026-08-07-distlib-deps-sidecar-under-reports.md`.
**Severity:** Medium — it does not corrupt output, but it hands consumers a **wrong** dep list that
looks authoritative, and the resulting failure lands in another repo on another target.

## What it claims to be

`dist/setu.deps` is generated by `cyrius distlib` and consumed by `cyrius deps`. Its own header says:

```
# cyrius dep sidecar - stdlib leaves this fold requires in scope.
```

## What it actually emits

For setu 0.8.2 it emits **8** leaves — `string fmt alloc vec str io syscalls assert` — while setu's
`[deps] stdlib` declares **12**. The four it omits are `result`, `net`, `chrono`, `args`.

## The three things that were MEASURED, not assumed

Each is a controlled experiment against `cyrius distlib`, reverted afterwards (`dist/setu.cyr` md5
returned to `cc0874ddb9ba0859356eefdc371d794e` each time, so nothing here changed the bundle):

1. ⛔ **It is NOT the declaration.** Deleting `"assert"` from `[deps] stdlib` and re-running `distlib`
   left `assert` **still in the sidecar**. The generator does not read the declared list at all.
2. ⛔ **It is NOT an inline-comment parse bug.** The first hypothesis was that entries carrying a
   trailing `#` comment get dropped — the four missing ones all have them, the eight survivors did not.
   Adding a comment to `"vec"` and regenerating left `vec` **present**. Hypothesis falsified.
3. ⛔ **It is NOT catching directly-referenced symbols either.** All four omitted modules are referenced
   at top level in `src/client.cyr`, not behind anything exotic: `tcp_socket` (`net`, lines 177/200),
   `is_ok` / `result_unwrap` (`result`), `sys_sleep_ms` (`chrono`), `getenv` (`args`, line 122).

So the sidecar is a **core subset**, not a derivation of either the declaration or the fold's symbols.

## Why it costs real time

`args` is the sharp case. setu's agnos arm calls `getenv` to read `AGNOS_CHAN` (the fd the compositor
endowed it). On agnos `io.cyr`'s `getenv` **delegates** to `args_agnos.cyr`'s `_agnos_getenv`, because
agnos has no `/proc`. A consumer that builds its `[deps] stdlib` from the sidecar — which is exactly
what the sidecar exists for — gets 8 leaves, compiles on Linux, and fails to link `_agnos_getenv` on
agnos. **Verified against a throwaway consumer built with exactly those 8.**

⛔ The failure surfaces in the *consumer*, on the *other target*, as an undefined symbol with no
pointer back to the sidecar that caused it. A wrong list that looks authoritative is worse than no
list: it is what a reader checks *instead of* the declaration.

## Ask

Either make `distlib` emit the declared `[deps] stdlib` verbatim (simplest, and matches what consumers
actually need), or make it a real transitive symbol closure that follows cross-module delegation like
`io.getenv` → `args._agnos_getenv`. Until then the header comment overstates it.

## Interim rule for consumers

⚠ **Treat `dist/*.deps` as a starting point, never a contract.** Take the lib's own `[deps] stdlib`
from its `cyrius.cyml`. setu 0.8.2 consumers need all twelve, and `args` in particular must not be
dropped from `aethersafha` or `crab`.
